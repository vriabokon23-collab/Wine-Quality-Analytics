Wine Quality Analytics & Predictive Modelling¶

1. Introduction¶

This report will analyse the combined dataset of white and red wine (winequality-combined.csv) to identify the key characteristics of wine quality. By understanding the key drivers of wine quality, producers and exporters can have a more complete picture when making pricing decisions and product positioning on international markets, as well as ensuring a more consistent and effective process of quality control. By looking at the measurable chemical attributes of wine, this analysis will aim to provide evidence-based insights into which wine content variables (such as volatile acidity, sugar levels and alcohol percentage) are most strongly linked to a higher quality wine.

The dataset that will be used in this analysis contains mixed observations of both white and red wine, encoded to 0 and 1 in binary respectively. This will ensure the quality of the report is consistent and more presentable. The dataset includes a wide range of chemical properties; such as sulphate levels, alcohol content (%) and acidity levels, along with a quality rating for each wine. This will form the basis of analysis of wine quality as well as the statistical modelling of quality.

This report will be structured as follows. Section 2 serves as an overview of the database, as well as imports all the relevant libraries that will be used for analysis in sections 3 and 4. Section 3 will serve as the analysis and examination of the relationship between the wine's various chemical properties and its quality rating. Section 4 will model such relationships into a visual representation of the data, as well as generate predictive insights. Finally, Section 5 will summarise all findings and discuss their implications onto a real-world context for wine makers and exporters.

For the quality of the presentation, the binary wine_type variable is mapped into readable wine labels of "White wine" and "Red wine" under a new variable of wine_label. The new variable will be used for the quality of the presentation when creating tables and graphs, whilst the original binary variable will be saved for statistical modelling in Section 4.

The selected quality predictors are chosen as: alcohol, volatile acidity, citric acid, and sulphates.

It is important to note that the correlation matrix only captures simple relationships between two variables. This means that although the alcohol and acidity levels are correlated to quality, the matrix cannot determine how important one variable is when other variables are taken into account. As a result, regression analysis is needed to better understand the contribution of every chemical variable to the wine quality.

A stronger test is conducted using a multiple regression model, as it examines several variables simultaneously, including alcohol, volatile acidity, citric acid, sulphates, and wine_type. This allows each coefficient to be interpreted whilst holding the other variables constant. Alcohol remains positive with a coefficient of 0.337, suggesting that it continues to be an important predictor of wine quality even after controlling for the other chemical characteristics.

In contrast, volatile acidity has a negative coefficient of approximately -1.5, implying that a one-unit increase in volatile acidity is associated with an estimated 1.5-point decrease in predicted wine quality, holding the other variables constant. This suggests that higher acidity levels are associated with lower-quality wines.

The R-squared value represents the proportion of variation in wine quality explained by the regression model. Although the model captures several important relationships, a substantial proportion of variation remains unexplained. Specifically, an R-squared value of approximately 0.29 suggests that around 29% of the variation in wine quality is explained by the selected variables, whilst roughly 71% remains unexplained. This implies that additional factors beyond the variables included in the model may also influence wine quality.

4.2.1 Interpretation of the Multiple Regression Model¶

The multiple regression results suggest that alcohol and sulphates are positively associated with wine quality, while volatile acidity is negatively associated with quality. Alcohol demonstrates a positive and statistically significant coefficient (β = 0.337, p < 0.001), implying that wines with higher alcohol content tend to receive higher predicted quality ratings, holding other variables constant. In contrast, volatile acidity displays the strongest negative coefficient (β = -1.546, p < 0.001), suggesting that excessive acidity substantially reduces predicted wine quality.

Sulphates also exhibit a positive and statistically significant relationship with quality. However, citric acid does not appear statistically significant (p = 0.530), implying that it provides limited explanatory power once the effects of the other variables are considered simultaneously. Finally, wine_type remains statistically significant after controlling for the selected chemical characteristics, although its coefficient is relatively modest, suggesting that wine category is a secondary predictor compared with measurable chemical composition.

From a business perspective, this suggests that although red and white wines differ chemically, quality monitoring and product positioning should focus more strongly on measurable chemical composition than wine category alone.

Although several variables such as alcohol and volatile acidity are statistically significant, statistical significance does not necessarily imply a large practical effect. The coefficient magnitudes should therefore be interpreted alongside their business relevance and the scale of each variable. For example, these findings may help producers make decisions relating to production methods, ingredient composition, grape cultivation, and quality control processes.

5. Conclusion¶

Overall, the analysis suggests that wine quality is associated more strongly with measurable chemical characteristics than with wine_type alone. The analysis identified systematic compositional differences between red and white wines; however, these differences were insufficient to fully explain the variation in quality ratings. In particular, alcohol appears to be a reliable positive predictor of wine quality, while volatile acidity is negatively associated with quality.

From a business perspective, these findings suggest that producers and exporters should monitor the chemical characteristics of wine as part of quality control and product positioning strategies. However, the analysis should be interpreted cautiously, as the models and dataset do not capture several other potentially important predictors of wine quality; such as grape variety, production methods, consumer preferences, and geographic origin. Further analysis could improve the model by testing additional predictors and comparing model performance separately across red and white wines.
